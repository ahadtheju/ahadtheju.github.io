Step by step

An order of the events

You are not expected to sit through all of it and nobody minds if you walk around, socialise, chat with people or go for coffee or food that is served in a different floor. Tap any underlined word to see what it means.

1 of 12

Step 1

Welcoming the groom

Mappillai Azhaippu, or Janavasam

The groom arrives and the bride's family receives him and his family and friends with a lamp waved in welcome (: a lit lamp circled in front of someone as a greeting and a blessing) and music from the (a long reed horn, the loudest thing at a South Indian wedding, played with drums).

Step 2

The groom pretends to run away

Kashi Yatra

The groom announces he is leaving for Kashi (Varanasi) to study and live as an unmarried student (brahmachari). He sets off with an umbrella, a walking stick, a fan, slippers and a book, usually the Bhagavad Gita. The bride's father stops him and asks him to marry his daughter instead. He (hopefully) agrees to stay.

It is played for laughs and the rest of the events happen if the groom chooses married life (grihastha) over a life of study alone.

Step 3

Exchanging garlands

Maalai Maatral

The couple exchanges flower garlands three times. Usually the maternal uncles hoist them onto their shoulders to make it harder, and both sides cheer. In our case that job falls to some of you.

It means the two of them accept each other, and the two families join.

Step 4

The swing, without a swing

Oonjal, and Pachai Pidi Suthal

Normally the couple sits on a swing (oonjal) while married women sing songs, the lullabies sung to rock the couple. We do not have a swing, so we will sit on two chairs.

  • Paal pazham: the couple is fed milk and banana.
  • Pachai pidi suthal: married women circle small rice balls, coloured red and yellow with (red powder used in rituals) and turmeric, around the couple, then throw them away in four directions to take the with them, the bad luck an envious look is said to leave behind.

Step 5

Asking permission to begin

Ashirvadam and Sankalpam

The start of the main ceremony. The parents ask the elders in the room for their blessing and their permission to begin. The priest (vadhyar) opens with a short prayer to clear obstacles.

Then the Sankalpam, a formal statement of intent where the priest announces the date by the (the traditional almanac that fixes dates and auspicious hours), the place, the names, and what everyone is here to do.

Step 6

Setting up the pot of water

Kalasa Avahanam

A pot (kalasam, or kumbam) is filled with water. Mango leaves go in the mouth of it and a coconut sits on top, often wrapped in cloth or thread. The priest invites the gods into it, mainly Varuna, the god of water.

That water gets sprinkled later to purify the place and everyone in it, a step called : literally the declaration of a holy day.

Step 7

Costume change and the lighting of fire

Koorai Pudavai and Agni Sandhanam

The groom's side gives the bride a silk saree (koorai pudavai) and she goes to change into it. Similarly the groom also changes into clothes he recieves from the bride's family.

While she is away the priest lights the sacred fire. From here on the fire is the witness to everything that follows (: agni is fire, sakshi is witness. Vows are made in front of it rather than signed).

Step 8

Blessing the thali

Mangalya Puja

The is the pendant the groom ties around the bride's neck., strung on its yellow thread (manjal kayiru), is set on a plate with a coconut, turmeric, kumkum and flowers, and prayed over.

The plate is then carried around the hall so the elders can touch it and bless it. If it comes past you, this is the moment to just do as others do.

Step 9

Tying the thali

Mangalya Dharanam

This is the main part of the ceremony. The bride sits on her father's lap. At the hour chosen as auspicious (: the window the almanac marks as the lucky time. Everything else bends around it) the starts up, a loud burst of horns and drums that tells the whole hall to look now.

The groom ties the thali while the priest chants Maangalyam tantunaanena, and guests shower the couple with raw rice mixed with turmeric and flowers (akshadhai).

The groom ties the first knot. The groom's sister (or a stand-in) ties the second and third.

Step 10

Seven steps, and the toe rings

Saptapadi and Metti

The groom holds the bride's right toe and guides her through seven steps beside the fire. She takes the steps, he guides. Each one carries a vow: food, strength, wealth, happiness, children and family, health through every season, and friendship for life.

Straight after, the bride puts her right foot on a grinding stone (ammi) by the fire and the groom slides silver toe rings (metti) onto the second toe of each foot. The stone signifies that the couple be firm like this stone in hard times. The toe rings, like the thali is worn by women who are married.

Step 11

The brother's handful of rice

Laja Homam, or Pori Iduthal

The bride's brother, or whoever is standing in for him, tips puffed rice (pori) into her cupped hands, with the groom's hands under hers. Together they offer it into the fire, usually three times.

It is a prayer for the groom's long life and for the family to do well. The brother's part in it says that the bride's family has not handed her over and stepped back. They are still behind her.

Step 12

The last blessing

Akshadhai Asirvadam

Akshadhai is raw rice mixed with turmeric, sometimes with kumkum. The word means unbroken, which is the whole wish: grains that have not been split, for a marriage that does not break.

The elders sprinkle it over the couple's heads, and the couple bows to them.

Afternoon

Valayadal / Nalangu

A lighthearted, joyous tradition with friendly competition and music. Guests are encouraged to pick a side and cheer them on!
